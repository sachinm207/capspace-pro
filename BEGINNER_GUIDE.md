## 1. What Problem Is This App Solving?

### In Most Sports / Video Games:
If Team A wants a player from Team B, they just agree on a trade or pay money, and the deal is done.

### In the NBA (Real Life):
Teams **cannot** just trade whoever they want. To prevent billionaire team owners from buying all the best superstars and ruining fair competition, the league created a strict **676-page legal rulebook** called the **Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA)**.

Under this rulebook:
1. Every team has a **spending limit (Salary Cap)**.
2. When two or three teams trade players, **the total dollar value of the incoming contracts must almost exactly balance the outgoing contracts**.
3. If a trade is off by even a few thousand dollars, or if a high-spending team takes back $1 more than they give away, **the league office legally blocks the trade**.

## 2. Why Existing Trade Machines (Like ESPN & Fanspo) Fail

Millions of basketball fans, sports journalists, and agents use online "Trade Machines" every day to test trade ideas.

### The Fatal Flaw of Existing Tools:
When you build a 3-team trade in existing tools and click "Test Trade", the website only does one thing:
* It flashes a giant, frustrating red banner:  
  ❌ **"TRADE FAILED: Incoming salary exceeds allowed threshold by $3,450,000."**

### And then... it leaves you in a dead end.
Existing websites do **not** tell you how to fix it. To solve that $3.45M deficit by hand, a human has to:
* Open 29 other team rosters in 29 separate browser tabs.
* Search for a 3rd team that has an unused "Traded Player Exception" (TPE) big enough to absorb the extra money.
* Check if that 3rd team has an open roster spot (teams can only have 15 players).
* Check if taking that player accidentally pushes the 3rd team over their own tax limit.

**95% of people give up** because solving a 3-team puzzle with 50 interdependent rules by hand takes 45 minutes of tedious spreadsheet math.

## 4. Key Terms Explained in Plain English

| Term | Simple Definition | Everyday Analogy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Salary Cap** | Baseline league spending target ($140.6M). | Target monthly budget. |
| **Luxury Tax** | Penalty zone ($170.8M) where owners pay cash fines for every dollar over. | Overdraft / penalty fee. |
| **First Apron** | High-spending checkpoint ($178.1M) where trade rules tighten. | High credit warning zone. |
| **Second Apron** | Super-tax zone ($188.9M) where trade rules are severely locked down. Cannot combine salaries or take back $1 extra. | Maximum credit limit lockdown. |
| **Salary Matching** | Exact piecewise mathematical formula dictating how much incoming contract money a team can accept. | Strict equal-value exchange rule. |
| **TPE (Trade Exception)** | A temporary financial credit coupon created when a team trades away a player without taking one back. | Store credit / gift voucher. |
| **Facilitator (3rd Team)** | A 3rd team with spare budget space that steps into a trade to absorb extra contract money in exchange for draft picks. | A co-signer / mediator. |
| **Protected Player** (🔒 Lock) | Superstars (like Steph Curry) marked as "untouchable" so the solver never trades them away. | Prized family heirloom. |
| **Roster Limit** | Maximum 15 players allowed on a team. (The app displays the top 6–9 core rotation players representing 85–90% of payroll in a scrollable box for clean UX). | 15 seats on the team bus. |
