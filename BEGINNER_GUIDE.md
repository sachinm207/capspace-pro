# CapSpace Pro: The Layman's Beginner Guide to NBA Trades & Salary Cap Rules

> **Who is this guide for?**  
> Anyone who knows nothing about basketball or the NBA, but wants to understand what this app does, why trading players is so difficult in real life, and what all the terms mean in simple everyday English.

## 1. What Problem Is This App Solving?

### In Most Sports / Video Games:
If Team A wants a player from Team B, they just agree on a trade or pay money, and the deal is done.

### In the NBA (Real Life):
Teams **cannot** just trade whoever they want. 

To prevent billionaire team owners from buying all the best superstars and ruining fair competition, the league created a strict **676-page legal rulebook** called the **Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA)**.

Under this rulebook:
1. Every team has a **spending limit (Salary Cap)**.
2. When two or three teams trade players, **the total dollar value of the incoming contracts must almost exactly balance the outgoing contracts**.
3. If a trade is off by even a few thousand dollars, or if a high-spending team takes back $1 more than they give away, **the league office legally blocks the trade**.

### What CapSpace Pro Does:
General Managers (GMs) of basketball teams spend weeks trying to do this complex financial math by hand. **CapSpace Pro** acts as an instant financial calculator and puzzle solver:
* You pick the players you want to acquire and protect your favorite superstars.
* The app automatically computes all the legal formulas, finds 3rd-party teams to help balance the money, and presents a 100% legal trade in seconds.

## 2. Key Terms Explained in Plain English

Here are the basketball and financial terms used in the app, explained without any complicated jargon:

---

### 🏛️ 1. Salary Cap ($140.6 Million)
* **What it means:** The baseline budget target set by the league for a team's total player payroll for one season.
* **Analogy:** Think of it as a household monthly budget. You can exceed it under special conditions, but doing so triggers financial penalties.

---

### 💸 2. Luxury Tax ($170.8 Million)
* **What it means:** If an owner spends more than the salary cap and crosses this line, they have to pay a hefty "fine" (tax) to the league for every extra dollar spent.
* **Why it matters:** Rich teams can afford to pay this tax to build strong championship rosters, but crossing it starts restricting how they can trade.

---

### 🚧 3. The "First Apron" ($178.1 Million)
* **What it means:** A stricter spending checkpoint above the luxury tax line.
* **The Rule:** Once a team crosses this threshold, the league takes away several trading conveniences. For example, they cannot take back more money than they send out in a trade.

---

### ⛔ 4. The "Second Apron" ($188.9 Million) — *The Strict Super-Tax*
* **What it means:** The highest and most restrictive financial penalty zone in basketball.
* **The Rules for Second Apron Teams:**
  1. **Strict 100% Salary Matching:** They cannot take back even $1 more incoming salary than they send out.
  2. **No "Aggregation":** They cannot combine two smaller player salaries together to trade for one bigger star.
  3. **No Cash Allowed:** They cannot include money to sweeten a trade.
* **Why it matters in the app:** When you see a team marked with the orange **`SECOND APRON`** badge, trades involving them fail 95% of the time unless the trade math is engineered with surgical precision.

---

### ⚖️ 5. Salary Matching (Piecewise Brackets)
* **What it means:** When teams trade players, they aren't just trading athletes—they are trading legally binding multi-million dollar annual contracts.
* **How it works:**
  * If a budget team trades away an $8M player, the rules let them receive up to $15.5M back (to help rebuilding teams).
  * If a rich luxury-tax team trades away a $30M star, they can only take back around $33.25M.
  * If a Second Apron team trades a $40M star, they can take back **at most $40.0M** (zero room for error).

---

### 🎫 6. Trade Exception / TPE (Traded Player Exception)
* **What it means:** A financial credit voucher.
* **How teams get it:** If Team A trades a $10M player to Team B without taking any player back, Team A receives a $10M "credit coupon" that lasts for one year.
* **How it is used:** Team A can use this credit voucher later to acquire a $10M player from another team without needing to give up any player salary in return.

---

### 🤝 7. Facilitator (The "Third Team")
* **What it means:** Often, Team A and Team B want to make a deal, but their math doesn't work out. Team A's player makes $35M, but Team B can only send back $25M.
* **The Solution:** They bring in **Team C (a Facilitator)**—usually a young team with lots of open budget space or a large TPE voucher. Team C absorbs the extra $10M player/contract in exchange for future draft picks, making the whole 3-way trade legally valid.

---

### 🔒 8. Protected Players (The "Do Not Touch" Lock)
* **What it means:** In the app, you will see a small lock icon next to franchise cornerstones (like Steph Curry or Jayson Tatum).
* **Why it's there:** A General Manager wants to trade bench pieces or secondary starters to improve the team, but would **never** want the trade solver to give away their franchise icon. Locking a player tells the algorithm: *"Make the math work, but keep this player on my roster."*

---

### 👥 9. Roster Limit (15 Standard Contracts)
* **What it means:** An NBA team can have a maximum of 15 players on standard NBA contracts at any time.
* **The Rule:** If a trade results in a team having 16 players, the trade is illegal until they cut (waive) someone or send extra players out.
* **Why the app lists 6 to 9 players per team:** Real NBA trades almost exclusively involve the top 6–9 core rotation players who earn ~85–90% of the team's total salary. Listing these players in a scrollable box keeps the 3-team trade board clean and easy to use without endless scrolling.
